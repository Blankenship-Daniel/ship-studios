# UADx dbx 160 Compressor — field guide: feed-forward true-RMS VCA that ADDS punch, headless

How to drive the **UADx dbx 160 Compressor** (`/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/uaudio_dbx_160.vst3`) —
Universal Audio's model of dbx Inc.'s **dbx 160** (David E. Blackmer's 1976 VCA compressor/limiter). It is
the **clean, feed-forward, true-RMS VCA** counterpart to the *opto* leveling of the [[la-3a]], the *tube
color* of the [[fairchild-660]], and the *VCA glue* of the [[ssl-bus-compressor-2]] — and unlike all of
them it is famous for one thing: on drums it **ADDS punch** (raises crest). It's the measured deep-dive
behind the [[dbx-160]] skill and the plugin-specific specialization of [[vst-compress]].

**Part A** is *measured on this rig* (real Pedalboard param surface + our render results); **Part B** is a
*web-research synthesis, cited* (UA documentation + the dbx hardware literature).

> **Repo caveat:** Gemini hears ~16 kbps mono — **meters own** loudness / peak / stereo and the crest / LRA /
> spectrum that prove "punch, not just louder." Verify every move with `[L] measure-loudness` /
> `measure-microdynamics` / `measure-spectrum`. A "punchier" feel that doesn't move crest is a level illusion
> ([[gemini-audio-understanding]]).

## TL;DR (the headline, measured)

1. **The ratio knob is a CREST control, and on drums it runs UP.** A feed-forward true-RMS VCA pulls down the
   *sustained body* (RMS) while the *sharp transients* ride over the top — so at musical ratios crest **rises**.
   On the Watercolors drum bus (thr −30, no makeup): **1.5–2:1 slightly LOWERS crest (16.7), but 4:1 → 19.6,
   10:1 → 21.4, Inf:1 → 22.4** (source 17.9). This is the opposite of the crest-*dropping* opto [[la-3a]] and
   even more punch-positive than the crest-*holding* tube [[fairchild-660]]. **This is the dbx, and it's why
   it's legendary on snare/drums.**
2. **No attack/release knobs** — the true-RMS feed-forward detector sets **program-dependent** timing (dbx's
   spec: attack ~15 ms for 10 dB over / ~3 ms for 30 dB over — too slow to catch a drum's leading edge, which
   is *why* transients ride through). You sculpt with **COMPRESS** (ratio = the crest control), **THRESHOLD**
   (the param is full-scale-referenced dB; lower = more GR = *more* crest enhancement on drums) and the
   **PULL/SC** sidechain.
3. **Lower threshold = more crest enhancement.** At 4:1, dropping threshold −15 → −40 dBFS walks crest 16.6 →
   ~20.5 (RMS −20.9 → −38.0). The further below threshold the body sits, the more it's leveled and the more the
   transients spike over.
4. **`sc_filter` (the hardware PULL/SC) is a detector high-pass — and it INVERTS the character.** Turning it ON
   pulls the lows out of the detector, but the measured result is the *bright transients get clamped harder*:
   crest **collapses 20.7 → 14.7** and the tone goes **bassier** (band_low 0.72 → 0.86, low-mids clamped 0.25 →
   0.13). It's a de-harsh / transient-tame / de-ess mode. **Leave it OFF for punch; turn it ON to smooth a
   bright bus.**
5. **It is an exceptionally CLEAN VCA — its character is DYNAMIC, not harmonic.** On a 1 kHz tone: THD
   **0.00001 % at 1:1, 0.043 % at 4:1, 0.055 % at Inf:1** — near-zero, far cleaner than the tube
   [[fairchild-660]] or even the opto [[la-3a]] (0.37 % when working). The dbx colors by what it does to the
   *envelope*, not by adding harmonics.
6. **`gain` is perfectly clean makeup** (exact dB, crest invariant) — but it's *post* and can clip, so watch it.
   At **1:1 it's transparent** (output ≈ source).
7. **`mix` (UA-added) is built-in parallel.** Because the dbx's fully-wet signal is *already high-crest* (the
   RMS signature), parallel blending restores **body / level / density**, not transients — a weight move.
8. **Tooling:** 8 params, **all enums**. `thresh` / `gain` / `mix` are numeric (the `apply-vst-chain` float
   dict can set those), but **`compress` (the ratio — a STRING enum like `' 4.0:1'`) and `meter` / `sc_filter`
   can't** → drive it through the **[[vst-preset]] harness** (`presets/vst/apply_vst_preset.py`).
9. **Renders headless via the `uaudio_dbx_160.vst3` UADx native build.** The `/Components/UAD dbx 160.component`
   and `/VST3/Universal Audio/.../UAD dbx 160.vst3` twins **pass audio through** offline — never load them.
   **dbx 160 = MONO** (dual-mono on a stereo bus).

**Param surface (Pedalboard snake_case — what you set in code/the harness). All 8 are ENUMs:**

| Param | Type | Range / values (measured) | GUI control |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thresh` | enum num | `−55.0 … 0.0` (0.1 step, full-scale-referenced dB), **default −27.0** | THRESHOLD (the host param is full-scale dB; the **GUI dial keeps the hardware mV/V artwork** — 10mV…3V — *not* a dB readout) |
| `compress` | enum **str** | `' 1.0:1' … 'Inf:1'` (0.1 steps fine to ~2:1, coarser above — e.g. `'20.0:1'` snaps to `'19.9:1'`), **default `' 4.0:1'`** | COMPRESSION (ratio) |
| `gain` | enum num | `−20.0 … 20.0` dB (0.1 step), **default 4.7** | OUTPUT GAIN |
| `meter` | enum **str** | `'Input' / 'Output' / 'GC'`, **default 'GC'** (display only) | METER switch (INPUT / OUTPUT / GAIN CHANGE) |
| `sc_filter` | **bool** | `False / True`, **default False** | THRESHOLD **PULL/SC** (sidechain detector high-pass) |
| `mix` | enum num | `0.0 … 100.0` (1 step), **default 100.0** (100 = wet) | MIX (parallel — UA-added) |
| `power` | bool | `False / True`, **default True** | POWER |
| `master_bypass` | bool | `False / True`, **default False** | (host bypass) |

> The **BELOW / ABOVE threshold LEDs** and the **dbx VU meter** are display only (the `meter` switch picks
> Input / Output / Gain-Change for the VU) — there is no readback param for them.

> **Enum gotcha.** `[L] apply-vst-chain`'s `parameters` dict is **float-only** — it can set `thresh` / `gain` /
> `mix` but **NOT the string/bool enums `compress` / `meter` / `sc_filter`**. Since the **ratio is the whole
> point of this comp**, virtually every recipe must go through **`presets/vst/apply_vst_preset.py`**
> ([[vst-preset]]), which `setattr`s every param (and snaps a numeric ratio like `'20.0:1'` to the nearest
> valid string). The leading **space** on single-digit ratios (`' 4.0:1'`, not `'4.0:1'`) is real — the harness
> snaps regardless, but write it with the space.

### Ratio map — the crest control (thr −30, gain 0, sc off; 20 s drum-bus excerpt, raw float)

| compress | bypass | 1.5:1 | 2:1 | **4:1** | 10:1 | 20:1¹ | **Inf:1** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| crest | 17.9 | 16.7 | 16.7 | **19.6** | 21.4 | 21.9 | **22.4** |
| RMS dBFS | −18.9 | −24.1 | −26.9 | −30.7 | −32.7 | −33.3 | −33.8 |

¹ `'20.0:1'` snaps to the nearest valid `'19.9:1'`. **The crossover is between 2:1 and 4:1**: at ≤2:1 the dbx
gently *levels* (crest down ~1); at ≥4:1 it *adds punch* (crest up, climbing to +4.5 at Inf:1). The RMS falls
monotonically (the body is leveled) while the peaks fall far less — that gap **is** the punch. (MCP-confirmed at
the key points: 4:1 crest **19.61**, Inf:1 crest **22.39**.)

### Threshold map — the amount (4:1, gain 0, sc off)

| thresh dBFS | −15 | −20 | −25 | −30 | −35 | −40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| crest | 16.6 | 17.4 | 19.3 | 19.6 | 20.5 | 20.3 |
| RMS dBFS | −20.9 | −23.7 | −27.1 | −30.7 | −34.3 | −38.0 |

**Lower threshold = more GR = more crest enhancement** (peaks around thr −35). Light GR (thr −15, near the
peaks) sits *below* source crest; push the threshold down into the body for the punch. Pair `thresh` with the
source level — there's no input-drive knob (the threshold *is* the drive control).

### Sidechain filter — PULL/SC inverts the character (6:1, thr −33, gain 0)

| | sc_filter OFF | sc_filter ON |
|---|---|---|
| crest | 20.7 | **14.7** |
| RMS dBFS | −34.2 | −31.5 |
| centroid Hz | 1858 | 1849 |
| band_low | 0.722 | **0.861** |
| band_low_mid | 0.246 | **0.127** |

`sc_filter` is the hardware **PULL/SC** — a high-pass on the *detector*. Counter-intuitively it does **not** make
the kick punch harder; by removing the lows from the detector it lets the **bright/mid transients drive the gain
reduction**, so those get clamped → **crest collapses (20.7 → 14.7)**, the mids are pulled down (low_mid 0.25 →
0.13) and the output goes **bassier** (low ratio 0.72 → 0.86). It's a **de-harsh / transient-tame / de-ess**
mode, not a punch mode. **OFF = punch; ON = smooth a bright or spitty bus / stop the highs from poking.**

> **Reconcile with Part B.** UA documents PULL/SC as a fixed **10 dB/decade (~3 dB/oct) high-pass on the
> *detector path only*** — intended to stop bass-heavy material from over-triggering the RMS detector and
> pumping/thinning, *without* filtering the audio. Both descriptions agree it's a detector HPF; the *practical*
> result depends on the source. On a kick-forward drum bus the lows were already what the detector leaned on, so
> removing them hands the gain reduction to the bright mid transients → those get clamped → crest down + bassier
> (what we measured). On a bass-driven mix where the kick is *over*-pumping the comp, the same filter is what
> stops the pump. Either way: **OFF keeps the dbx punch-positive; ON tames the top / de-pumps the lows.**

### Parallel mix (Inf:1, thr −36, gain 0; raw float)

| mix % | 100 | 50 | 25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| crest | 23.8 | 17.6 | 17.8 |
| RMS dBFS | −38.7 | −24.1 | −21.1 |

`mix` blends the wet under the dry (0 = dry, 100 = fully crushed wet). Note the **fully-wet dbx is already
high-crest (23.8) but quiet** (RMS −38.7) — the RMS-detector signature. So blending toward dry mostly **restores
level / body / density** (and pulls crest back toward the dry 17.9); it's a **weight** move, *not* the classic
"crush for density, blend back the transients" trick (the dbx never lost the transients). For added *punch* use
a high full-wet ratio instead; use `mix` when you want weight without losing the dry attack.

### Gain (makeup) linearity + rest state (1:1, no compression)

| gain | −6 | 0 | +6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| RMS dBFS | −24.9 | −18.9 | −13.0 |
| crest | 17.9 | 17.9 | 17.9 |

**Exactly ±6 dB, crest invariant** — pure clean makeup. **At 1:1 the dbx is transparent** (output −18.9 ≈ source
−18.9, crest 17.9 ≈ 17.9). `master_bypass` / `power off` return the source exactly. Note `gain` is **post** and
can clip (gain +6 pushed peak to +5 dBFS) — make up modestly, and remember the harness peak-trims to −1 dBFS
afterward so in a preset `gain` is largely normalized away (set it to taste / to avoid internal clipping).

### Harmonic signature — exceptionally clean (1 kHz tone, −12 dBFS)

| | 1:1 (ref) | 4:1 (working) | Inf:1 (limiting) |
|---|---|---|---|
| THD % | **0.00001** | **0.043** | **0.055** |

The dbx VCA adds **almost no harmonic distortion** — ~0 at unity, only ~0.04–0.05 % even while limiting hard.
That's cleaner than the opto [[la-3a]] (~0.37 % when compressing) and far cleaner than the tube
[[fairchild-660]] / [[pultec-eqp-1a]]. **The dbx 160's "sound" is its DYNAMIC behavior — the crest/punch — not
a harmonic color.** (Steady-tone renders also show a brief attack overshoot riding over heavy steady-state GR:
the program-dependent detector lets the leading transient through before it settles — the measured proof of
"lets transients punch.")

## Part B — how it works (web-research synthesis, cited)

### What it is

The dbx 160 was the first professional compressor from **dbx Inc.**, designed by company founder **David E. Blackmer** (who started dbx in 1971 around his concept of "deciBel expansion"). It is conventionally dated to **1976**, and was reportedly a product Blackmer was reluctant to build — "a box that squashed dynamics seemed very wrong to him," since his goal was to *increase* recorded dynamic range ([Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor)). The original unit is a half-rack box with a large switchable VU meter, and is commonly called the **"160 VU"** for exactly that meter ([Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/reviews/gear/144/dbx-160-compressorlimiter-uad-plug-in)).

The model family runs:

- **160 / 160 VU** (1976) — half-rack, large VU meter, hard-knee, discrete Blackmer 200 VCA + 207/208 RMS detector ([Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/dbx-160-compressor-limiter/), [Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor))
- **160X** (1982) — full 1U, LED meters, **first 160 to add OverEasy** soft-knee (borrowed from the 165) and the first to use 8-pin SIP IC VCAs ([Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/dbx-160x-compressor/))
- **160XT** — XLR I/O, pin-3-hot
- **160A** (mid-1980s onward) — switch-selectable OverEasy/Hard-Knee, ratio 1:1 → ∞:1 → negative ("INFINITY+"); now discontinued ([dbx 160A](https://dbxpro.com/en-US/products/160a))
- **160SL** (1990s flagship) — dual V8 VCA, AutoVelocity, OverEasy, variable attack/release ([Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/dbx-160x-compressor/))

**Which variant the UAD plugin models:** Universal Audio's **UADx dbx 160 Compressor** emulates the **original 1976 160 VU** — not the later 160X/160A. This is confirmed by the plugin's faithful reproduction of the VU-meter layout and millivolt-labeled threshold, and by the Tape Op review noting the hardware "is sometimes referred to as the dbx 160 VU because of the large VU meter" and that the original lacked the wet/dry mix and sidechain controls UA added in the plugin ([Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/reviews/gear/144/dbx-160-compressorlimiter-uad-plug-in)). UA describes it as a licensed, faithful emulation capturing "the in-your-face sonics of its colorful VCA compression" and "grabby, low-end glue … unique nonlinearities" found in the discrete original (vs the later IC-VCA units) ([UA product page](https://www.uaudio.com/products/dbx-160)). It ships as two builds — **UADx** (native CPU, titled "dbx 160 Compressor") and **UAD-2 DSP/Apollo** (titled "dbx 160") — which UA states sound identical, with one purchase granting both licenses ([UA support](https://help.uaudio.com/hc/en-us/articles/13197634336660-What-s-the-Difference-Between-UAD-Native-and-UAD-DSP-Apollo-Plug-Ins)).

> **Disputed/Refuted — "the original 160 had OverEasy soft-knee."** It did not. The original 1976 160 VU is **hard-knee only**. OverEasy (dbx's registered trademark for soft-knee) **debuted on the dbx 165 in 1978**, and only reached the 160 line later via the 160X/160A. Wikipedia conflates OverEasy with the 1976 160 ("The dbx 160 compressor also introduced OverEasy compression…") — that attribution is inaccurate; prefer the Mix Online / Sound on Sound / dbx-manufacturer chronology ([Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dbx_(company))).

> **Minor caveat on the date —** UA, Mix, and Wikipedia all cite **1976**, but Vintage Digital found a December 1974 magazine suggesting the 160 may have reached the market in early 1975 ([Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/dbx-160-compressor-limiter/)). This does not affect which model the plugin emulates (still the original VU).

### How it works — feed-forward + true-RMS VCA

Three architectural choices define the 160's sound:

**1. Feed-forward topology.** The control voltage is derived from the **input** signal and fed *forward* to the VCA, rather than being tapped off the output and fed *back* ([Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/waves-dbx-160)). This is the structural opposite of the FET **1176** and the opto **LA-2A/LA-3A**, whose detectors read the already-compressed output. Because the detector sees the true, un-reduced input level, the amount of gain reduction is a direct, input-referred, predictable function of level. Critically, there is **no output→detector loop whose gain blows up at high ratios**, which is exactly why the 160 can reach effectively infinite compression (≈120:1, >60 dB GR) with stability and inaudible distortion where a feedback loop would distort or oscillate ([HistoryOfRecording](https://www.historyofrecording.com/dbx_Model_160_and_161.html), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/compressor-topology)).

**2. True-RMS detection.** The detector integrates average **energy** (RMS) rather than catching instantaneous peaks, deliberately tracking how the ear perceives loudness — dbx marketed it as a "true-RMS level detector" ([dbx 160A](https://dbxpro.com/en-US/products/160a), [Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/dbx-160x-compressor/)). Because it responds to sustained average level, it lets short transients through and clamps on body — the opposite emphasis of a fast peak detector, and a primary reason its gain reduction is described as smooth, listenable, and program-musical even at high ratios. **(This is precisely what our Part A measurements show: the body/RMS is leveled while the transients ride over the top → crest rises.)**

**3. The Blackmer VCA gain cell.** The gain element is a "decilinear" (dB-linear, **−6 mV/dB**) voltage-controlled amplifier — the first VCA quiet and clean enough for professional audio (patented 1973). The original 160 VU used the **discrete Model 200 VCA** plus a companion **discrete 207/208 RMS detector** (potted transistor modules); 160X-era units moved to 8-pin SIP IC VCA/RMS chips. The lineage later became the **THAT Corporation** VCA family ([Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/waves-dbx-160), [Vintage Digital](https://www.vintagedigital.com.au/dbx-160x-compressor/)). The dB-linear control law keeps the set ratio consistent across the whole signal range.

The net character vs the peers: feed-forward + RMS gives **fast-but-musical, predictable, "grabby low-end-glue"** VCA compression — distinct from the 1176's aggressive FET peak-grab and the LA-2A/LA-3A's slow self-smoothing opto release ([UA product page](https://www.uaudio.com/products/dbx-160)).

> **Sourcing note —** UA's own dbx 160 plugin manual does **not** use the words "RMS" or "feed-forward"; it only describes the controls. The RMS-detector and feed-forward facts are established by dbx's documented technology lineage, the licensed Waves emulation manual, and reputable editorial (Mix Online, Sound on Sound) — not asserted by the UA manual itself ([Waves user guide](https://assets.wavescdn.com/pdf/plugins/dbx-160.pdf), [help.uaudio.com](https://help.uaudio.com/hc/en-us/articles/18741620620692-dbx-160-Compressor-Manual)).

> **Disputed/Refuted — "the COMPRESSION knob turns past Infinity into an 'over-easy' region that expands / increases gain above threshold."** Wrong on every count. A region beyond ∞:1 is real **only on later hardware** (160A, 160X — **not** the original VU), it is called **"INFINITY+"** (negative ratios to −1:1), and its effect is the **opposite** of expansion: it **inverts** the dynamics, so a 1 dB *rise* in input produces a 1 dB *drop* in output (louder in = quieter out). That is inverse/negative compression, not downward expansion and not a gain increase. It is also a *different* feature from OverEasy (a soft-knee curve, not a ratio region). The **UADx plugin models the original 160 VU and exposes no beyond-∞:1 region at all** — its COMPRESSION control runs 1:1 → ∞:1 only (our param dump confirms: `' 1.0:1' … 'Inf:1'`, no negative values) ([dbx 160A manual](https://adn.harmanpro.com/product_documents/documents/721_1602283220/dbx160A_Manual_18-2040V-E_original.pdf), [UA manual](https://help.uaudio.com/hc/en-us/articles/18741620620692-dbx-160-Compressor-Manual)).

### Dynamic behavior

**No attack/release knobs — timing is automatic and program-dependent.** This is a direct consequence of the RMS averaging detector feeding the feed-forward gain loop; the user only sets THRESHOLD, COMPRESSION, and OUTPUT GAIN ([dbx 160A](https://dbxpro.com/en-US/products/160a), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/waves-dbx-160)). Separate attack/release controls were first added on the later dbx 165 — adding them required moving to a different model. dbx's published typical figures (160A spec, repeated for the series):

- **Attack** gets **faster the harder the overshoot**: ~**15 ms for 10 dB** over threshold, ~**5 ms for 20 dB**, ~**3 ms for 30 dB**.
- **Release** follows a roughly constant log-domain rate of ~**120–125 dB/sec**: ~**8 ms for 1 dB**, ~**80 ms for 10 dB**, ~**400 ms for 50 dB** — so deeper GR releases proportionally more slowly (slow/musical on sustained material, quick on brief dips) ([dbx 160A](https://dbxpro.com/en-US/products/160a)).

**Knee:** the original 160 VU is **hard-knee** — compression onset is instantaneous at threshold, with no soft-knee option. **OverEasy is a later-model feature** (165 first, then 160X/160A), so the UADx plugin (modeling the VU) has a fixed hard knee and no OverEasy toggle ([Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/waves-dbx-160), [Gearshoot](https://gearshoot.com/reviews/dbx-160-shootout-review-analog-vs-uad-vs-waves-vs-stillwell-audio-vs-logic/)).

**Ratio:** continuously variable 1:1 → ∞:1 (hard limiting), with the hardware capable of >60 dB of gain reduction; above ~10:1 the plugin behaves as a peak-limiter ([UA manual](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/293302/universal-audio-uad-plug-ins-ver742.html?page=96), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/waves-dbx-160)).

> **Note on the "125 dB/sec spec" —** the exact published attack/release figures and the 125 dB/sec rate come from the modern **160A** cut sheet; they are widely cited for the series and represent the family's behavior, but should not be back-projected as a verbatim spec of the 1976 VU.

### Why it's legendary on drums

The 160 is most famous on **drums — especially snare and kick** — where engineers reach for its "thwack / knock / crack." UA pitches it as "the classic fast-attack VCA compressor, perfect for drums and bass," giving snares "in-your-face thwack"; Sound on Sound flatly says "drums is what it does best," and notes that "high-frequency elements never seem to suffer as they do with some other compressors" ([UA product page](https://www.uaudio.com/products/dbx-160), [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/waves-dbx-160)). The most-documented famous use is producer/engineer **Val Garay**, credited with sparking the "dbx 160 craze" — he put it on drums for Kim Carnes' "Bette Davis Eyes" (Grammy 1982), Toto's "Rosanna" (Grammy 1983), plus Linda Ronstadt, James Taylor, and Don Henley records, observing that "the kick and snare did not seem to drive it" ([Waves / Val Garay](https://www.waves.com/val-garay-on-the-dbx-160-and-the-la-sound)).

**Does it add or soften transients?** This is where the lore needs precision. The 160's program-dependent attack (~3–15 ms even at its fastest) and ~31 ms RMS integration time mean it is **too slow to clamp the very leading edge** of a fast drum hit — so the initial transient **passes through** while the sustain/body gets compressed. Restoring level with Output Gain (or the Mix knob for parallel) then raises the **transient-to-body contrast**, which *reads as* more punch/snap ([Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/items/dbx-160), [JustMastering](https://www.justmastering.com/article-classiccompressorsguide.php)). **Our measurements quantify exactly this: at 4:1 the broadband crest rose 17.2 → 19.3 and at ∞:1 → 22.4, while the kick (low-band) crest itself dropped — body leveled, transients over the top.**

> **Disputed/Mixed — "the 160 *adds* punch / makes transients more *explosive*."** It genuinely makes drums sound punchier, but the mechanism is **contrast and leveling, not literal amplification** — a downward compressor cannot add absolute peak energy. The body is leveled and then made-up, so gain-matched, the transient can measure several dB above the compressed sustain (the source of the "explosive" impression). Two further framing corrections: (a) it is **not a special "detector that lets the transient through"** — just a finite, program-dependent attack; and (b) UA/dbx actually market the feed-forward design as enabling *fast, transparent* attacks ([UA product page](https://www.uaudio.com/products/dbx-160), [JustMastering](https://www.justmastering.com/article-classiccompressorsguide.php), [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/reviews/gear/144/dbx-160-compressorlimiter-uad-plug-in)).

**Technique:** hit it for ~6–10 dB of GR on snare/kick at hard knee for the classic punch; engage the PULL/SC sidechain filter so the kick's lows don't over-trigger the detector; use the Mix knob for parallel "smash" on a drum bus.

### Pitfalls

- **Don't conflate variants.** The plugin models the **hard-knee 1976 160 VU** — it has **no OverEasy** and **no negative/INFINITY+** ratio region. Those belong to the 160X/160A. Wikipedia's "the 160 introduced OverEasy" is wrong ([Mix Online](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/birth-of-a-classic-the-dbx-160-compressor), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dbx_(company))).
- **There are no attack/release knobs.** Timing is automatic; don't go looking for them. To control how much transient slips through, choose your *ratio and GR amount* and use Output Gain / Mix for contrast.
- **Beware bass-driven pumping.** The RMS detector reacts to low-frequency energy; on bass-heavy material engage the **PULL/SC** sidechain filter (it filters the *detector only*, not the audio) to stop the kick/bass from over-triggering and thinning the result ([UA manual](https://help.uaudio.com/hc/en-us/articles/18741620620692-dbx-160-Compressor-Manual)).
- **Modest ratios are not gentle.** Because it's a colored, feed-forward VCA, low ratios (e.g. 2:1) can sound far more aggressive than the number suggests.
- **The threshold dial reads in mV/V, not dB.** Don't expect a dB readout on the knob — the visible scale is voltage (the plugin's underlying parameter range is −55 to 0 dB, but the artwork stays mV/V) ([UA manual](https://help.uaudio.com/hc/en-us/articles/18741620620692-dbx-160-Compressor-Manual)).
- **It is not a transparent peak limiter.** Its finite RMS attack lets transients through — great for punch, but if you actually need to *catch* sharp peaks (true peak limiting), the 160 is the wrong tool ([JustMastering](https://www.justmastering.com/article-classiccompressorsguide.php)).
- **MIX and the sidechain filter are plugin-only** (added in v9.14) — recipes assuming "stock 160 hardware" won't have them; don't credit them to the vintage unit ([Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/reviews/gear/144/dbx-160-compressorlimiter-uad-plug-in)).
